## Typography hierarchy

Typical hierarchy inferred from the deck:

- Cover title: ~28–36 pt equivalent depending on title length.
- Section title: ~32–45 pt, bold/semibold, high contrast.
- Standard slide title: ~20–24 pt, semibold/bold.
- Body: ~12–16 pt.
- Table/body-small: ~9–12 pt only when necessary.
- Footnotes/sources: ~7–9 pt; avoid going below readable size in normal presentation view.

## Composition rules

1. Keep content slides predominantly white.
2. Prefer one clear visual anchor per slide.
3. Use green table headers with white type for structured portfolio comparisons.
4. Avoid dense decorative card systems foreign to the reference deck.
5. Use section-color slides sparingly to reset the narrative.
6. Retain generous margins and avoid filling the page edge-to-edge unless using a full-bleed section/image slide.
7. Use photographs/scientific visuals only when they carry evidence or context; no decorative stock-photo treatment.
8. Use charts with restrained corporate palette and direct labels where practical.
9. Put citations/sources in a consistent bottom band above the corporate footer.
10. Keep slide numbers/footer elements controlled by the master/layout wherever possible.

## Management-deck adaptation

For Board/R&D/portfolio presentations, adapt the scientific reference style as follows without breaking the corporate identity:

- statement-led titles;
- fewer words per slide than technical decks;
- portfolio matrices, timelines and staged investment diagrams instead of large prose blocks;
- explicit `Decision`, `Risk`, `Gate` and `Assumption` labels;
- modeled assumptions visually distinguished from externally confirmed facts;
- final slide contains a specific requested decision, funding envelope and next gate.
